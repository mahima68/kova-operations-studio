# Kova Lifestyle — sample launch plan

Kova is a fictional portfolio brand selling men's and women's T-shirts in India. All prices, costs, customers and results are examples. No stock has actually been purchased and no campaign has run.

## 1. Define the first collection

Promise: comfortable everyday T-shirts with simple colours and clear size information.

Working audience: people aged roughly 18–30 looking for everyday casual clothing. This is a customer hypothesis to test, not completed market research.

| Decision | Sample choice |
| --- | --- |
| Styles | Everyday and Oversized |
| Fits | Men and Women |
| Colours | Ink, Chalk and Sage |
| Sizes | XS, S, M, L and XL |
| Total variants | 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 = 60 SKUs |
| First sales channels | Instagram, WhatsApp and a basic online store |
| Operations | One owner packing orders from home |

Keep real garment measurements, fabric composition, wash tests and supplier samples on file before selling. A fictional size label is not a tested size chart.

## 2. Cost and price the products

The app's starting unit costs are reference assumptions. They are not verified supplier quotes.

| Per item | Everyday | Oversized |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Selling price | ₹699 | ₹899 |
| Garment and print cost | ₹260 | ₹340 |
| Packaging assumption | ₹25 | ₹25 |
| Shipping assumption | ₹75 | ₹75 |
| Payment fee assumption | 2% | 2% |
| Estimated contribution | ₹325.02 | ₹441.02 |

Contribution above assumes one item per order, no customer shipping charge and no return. It is before operating expenses and tax. Actual multi-item orders share their recorded shipping and packaging costs.

Sample opening stock is 468 units, with 3 XS, 8 S, 12 M, 8 L and 8 XL units for each style/fit/colour group. Its reference product cost is ₹1,40,400. Adding ₹3,600 in sample operating expenses and a ₹16,000 planning reserve gives an illustrative starting amount of ₹1,60,000. This is a portfolio scenario, not a recommended amount to invest or a supplier quotation.

The separate open purchase order is a later replenishment example. Do not add its units to opening stock until it is received.

## 3. Prepare the business before launch

1. Compare written supplier quotes, sample quality, lead times and minimum order quantities.
2. Test fit and wash quality for both fits, then produce an accurate size chart.
3. Photograph the actual products and record colour, fabric and care details.
4. Check product costs and planned selling prices in the app.
5. Prepare packaging and a repeatable packing checklist.
6. Choose payment and courier services and test them outside this prototype.
7. Confirm applicable tax, invoice and selling requirements with a qualified adviser before real sales.
8. Write clear shipping, return and exchange terms and show them before checkout.
9. Run one full test order, including cancellation, return and refund.

## 4. First 30 days

| Days | Main work | Evidence to keep |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1–5 | Define audience, compare competing products and draft collection | Research notes and price comparison |
| 6–10 | Review samples, fit and supplier quotes | Photos, measurements and cost sheet |
| 11–15 | Prepare product pages, photos and packaging | Listing drafts and packing checklist |
| 16–20 | Test orders, payments, shipping and returns | Test log with problems and fixes |
| 21–25 | Launch the fictional campaign plan and prepare content | Draft posts, links and ad worksheet |
| 26–30 | Review sample sales, stock gaps and costs | Dashboard export and short decisions log |

For this portfolio, “launch” means showing the plan and a tested demo. Do not report real reach, customers, conversions or revenue.

## 5. Repeatable daily routine

- Check new enquiries and confirm product availability.
- Create the order to reserve stock before promising a delivery.
- Record payment received outside the app, or select COD.
- Check SKU, size, colour and quantity before packing.
- Record courier details and dispatch only when the parcel is sent.
- Confirm delivery and record any COD money received.
- Enter returns, restock only sellable items and record refunds separately.
- Review low-stock variants and order replenishment when needed.
- Enter expenses and save a backup regularly.

## 6. Proposed Google Ads test

The app includes a draft search plan, not an active Google Ads campaign.

- Ad groups: men's cotton T-shirts, women's cotton T-shirts and oversized T-shirts.
- Example ideas: “cotton t shirts for men”, “women oversized t shirt” and “oversized t shirts India”.
- Suggested exclusions for review: free, wholesale, jobs and printing service.
- Draft message: “Kova Lifestyle | Your Everyday Tee”.
- Landing-page checks: accurate photos, size chart, delivery charges, stock, care details and return terms.
- Example budget: ₹5,000 at an assumed ₹10 per click gives 500 clicks. An assumed 2% purchase rate gives 10 purchases in the calculator. These are assumptions, not predicted results.

For a real campaign, choose goals and measurement first, then verify setup and results in the chosen advertising platform. This prototype has no advertising connection.
